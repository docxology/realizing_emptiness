The criticality layer computes the two finite signatures the source paper names for its empirical criticality prediction, rather than a hand-tuned score, over seeded boundary-channel activity series drawn from the active-inference trajectories. A branching ratio is estimated as descendants per ancestor over each activity series, and an avalanche-size distribution is extracted from maximal supra-threshold runs of that activity and summarised by a finite power-law-versus-exponential log-likelihood-ratio diagnostic [@beggs2003neuronal]. The derived criticality index is the absolute distance of the measured branching ratio from one, so the keep-or-critical reading follows the measurement rather than a constant; the caution is that these signatures are not dispositive even in empirical neuroscience without stronger controls [@touboul2017power_law_absence_criticality; @destexhe2021criticality_evidence].

The engine carries both kinds of control. For branching, the discriminating negative control is a seeded permutation of each original observation series, preserving its histogram while disrupting temporal order. A profile's measured branching ratio is credited as separated only when its real and shuffled ninety-five percent confidence intervals are disjoint. The other trajectory metrics retain independently sampled uniform observation/action controls; their contrasts do not isolate temporal order at fixed marginals. The aggregate readiness gate requires a majority of profiles to separate on at least one signature and at least one profile to separate on measured branching. The positive control is a planted-branching calibration: a pure geometric activity series with a known branching ratio is fed to the estimator, which must recover the planted value within tolerance. This calibrates the finite estimator, not an empirical criticality claim. The effect-size forest separately reports profile-minus-random-null differences, run-level mean-difference and Cliff's-delta bootstrap intervals, and Holm-adjusted permutation tests.

These signatures structure the shape of a future empirical test; they are not neural measurements, clinical outcomes, or evidence that any contemplative practice produced a critical regime.

### Seeded Criticality Indicators with Null Controls {#sec:supplement-criticality-proxies}
The reported criticality-style outputs are seeded stochastic simulation indicators computed from the active-inference trajectories: observation entropy, action entropy, switching rate, variance, autocorrelation, null-control contrasts, and confidence intervals over replayable seeds. The single-trace diagnostic remains as a compact continuity check, but @fig:criticality_stochastic_ensemble carries the claim-bearing result because it exposes variability and null controls.

@fig:criticality_signatures shows that the measured branching ratio separates the profile ensembles from their shuffled null controls by disjoint ninety-five percent confidence intervals, which is the finite software analogue of a criticality signature and not a measurement of neural avalanches or branching in any nervous system. The avalanche-size power-law-versus-exponential log-likelihood ratio is reported for continuity only and is non-discriminating on a single trace, where a sample size of one leaves the power-law and exponential fits statistically indistinguishable; it is the branching-ratio separation against the shuffled null, not the single-trace avalanche fit, that carries the criticality reading. The surrogate fixes the shape of a future empirical test; it does not observe criticality.

The effect-size forest now estimates uncertainty for the contrasts themselves. For each profile and trajectory metric, independent complete runs are resampled separately in the two groups, and both the mean difference and tie-aware Cliff's delta are recomputed in each of 2,048 replicates. These are pointwise percentile intervals; they are not simultaneous coverage guarantees and timesteps are not independent replicates. Separate group and pooled-mean intervals remain in the JSON for traceability but do not supply the effect intervals in @fig:stochastic_effect_size_forest [@westfall1993resampling].

The two-sided mean-statistic permutation calculation uses 4,095 independent random label permutations with the plus-one correction, giving an attainable resolution of one over 4,096. Its validity as a test requires exchangeability under the null, a stronger assumption than equal means alone. The artifact records seeds, exceedances, draw counts, Monte Carlo standard errors, and exact binomial bounds. Bonferroni bounds across the declared eighteen-contrast family are propagated through the monotone Holm adjustment to flag decisions whose finite-permutation uncertainty straddles the threshold. This Monte Carlo uncertainty is separate from uncertainty across simulated trajectories; a nonrejection is not evidence of equality.

The branching comparison has a different sampling design. Each original trajectory is paired with its own fixed histogram-preserving shuffle, and the paired bootstrap resamples intact original/shuffle run pairs. @fig:criticality_paired_contrasts reports an interval for that difference without replacing the existing disjoint-interval gate. It conditions on one shuffle per run and does not integrate over all possible shuffle realizations. No paired sign-flip p-value is asserted, because pairing alone does not establish null sign symmetry.

Calibration now includes 128 independent seeded normal-generator repetitions in addition to the original null, injected-shift, and sign-flip fixtures. Each repetition records actual intervals and rejection/coverage outcomes for zero and unit shifts. Exact binomial intervals accompany the rejection and coverage frequencies in @fig:resampling_calibration; the nominal lines are references, not certificates. At zero or complete rejection the plug-in Monte Carlo standard error is zero, but the exact binomial interval still exposes finite-budget uncertainty. This is a limited generator diagnostic, not empirical power, universal type-I-error control, or Bayesian posterior calibration.
