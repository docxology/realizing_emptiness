The criticality layer computes two finite signatures over seeded boundary-channel activity series drawn from the active-inference trajectories. A branching ratio is estimated as descendants per ancestor over each activity series, and an avalanche-size distribution is extracted from maximal supra-threshold runs of that activity and summarised by a finite power-law-versus-exponential log-likelihood-ratio diagnostic [@beggs2003neuronal]. The derived criticality index is the absolute distance of the measured branching ratio from one. These signatures are not dispositive even in empirical neuroscience without stronger controls [@touboul2017power_law_absence_criticality; @destexhe2021criticality_evidence].

The engine carries both kinds of control. For branching, the discriminating negative control is a seeded permutation of each original observation series, preserving its histogram while disrupting temporal order. A profile's measured branching ratio is credited as separated only when its real and shuffled ninety-five percent confidence intervals are disjoint. The other trajectory metrics retain independently sampled uniform observation/action controls; their contrasts do not isolate temporal order at fixed marginals. The aggregate readiness gate requires a strict majority of profiles to separate on measured branching, independently reconstructed from the recorded trajectories and run summaries. The positive control is a planted-branching calibration: a pure geometric activity series with a known branching ratio is fed to the estimator, which must recover the planted value within tolerance. This calibrates the finite estimator, not an empirical criticality claim. The effect-size forest separately reports profile-minus-random-null differences, run-level mean-difference and Cliff's-delta bootstrap intervals, and Holm-adjusted permutation tests.

These signatures structure the shape of a future empirical test; they are not neural measurements, clinical outcomes, or evidence that any contemplative practice produced a critical regime.

The reported outputs distinguish temporal-order diagnostics from descriptive trajectory summaries. @fig:criticality_stochastic_ensemble reports finite software observation entropy and switching rate under independent random controls. Measured branching uses a histogram-preserving shuffled control in @fig:criticality_signatures.

@fig:criticality_signatures reports measured branching against shuffled null controls as a finite software diagnostic, not a measurement of neural avalanches or branching in any nervous system. The single-trace avalanche histogram is descriptive: when fewer than three sizes are available, the shape comparison returns zero as an insufficient-sample convention and cannot discriminate distributions. The branching-ratio separation against the shuffled null supplies the gated comparison. The surrogate fixes the shape of a future empirical test; it does not observe criticality.

The effect-size forest estimates uncertainty for the contrasts themselves. For each profile and trajectory metric, independent complete runs are resampled separately in the two groups, and both the mean difference and tie-aware Cliff's delta are recomputed in each of 2,048 replicates. These are pointwise percentile intervals; they are not simultaneous coverage guarantees and timesteps are not independent replicates. The intervals in @fig:stochastic_effect_size_forest match these contrast estimands [@westfall1993resampling].

The two-sided mean-statistic permutation calculation uses 4,095 independent random label permutations with the plus-one correction, giving an attainable resolution of one over 4,096. Its validity as a test requires exchangeability under the null, a stronger assumption than equal means alone. The artifact records seeds, exceedances, draw counts, Monte Carlo standard errors, and exact binomial bounds. Bonferroni bounds across the declared eighteen-contrast family are propagated through the monotone Holm adjustment to flag decisions whose finite-permutation uncertainty straddles the threshold. This Monte Carlo uncertainty is separate from uncertainty across simulated trajectories; a nonrejection is not evidence of equality.

The branching comparison has a different sampling design. Each original trajectory is paired with its own fixed histogram-preserving shuffle, and the paired bootstrap resamples intact original/shuffle run pairs. @fig:criticality_paired_contrasts reports an interval for that difference without replacing the existing disjoint-interval gate. It conditions on one shuffle per run and does not integrate over all possible shuffle realizations. No paired sign-flip p-value is asserted, because pairing alone does not establish null sign symmetry.

Calibration now includes 128 independent seeded normal-generator repetitions in addition to the original null, injected-shift, and sign-flip fixtures. Each repetition records actual intervals and rejection/coverage outcomes for zero and unit shifts. Exact binomial intervals accompany the rejection and coverage frequencies in @fig:resampling_calibration; the nominal lines are references, not certificates. At zero or complete rejection the plug-in Monte Carlo standard error is zero, but the exact binomial interval still exposes finite-budget uncertainty. This is a limited generator diagnostic, not empirical power, universal type-I-error control, or Bayesian posterior calibration.
